# Honeybee Colonies

A honeybee colony is a single, highly organized society that can grow to tens of thousands of bees at the height of summer. The bees fall into three types, called castes, and each caste has a distinct role:

- A single queen, the only fully reproductive female, whose main job is to lay eggs, often many hundreds in a day.
- Many female workers, which do not normally reproduce but carry out almost all of the hive's labor: foraging for nectar and pollen, building and cleaning comb, feeding the young, and defending the colony.
- A smaller number of male drones, whose main purpose is to mate with a queen.

Bees build their nest from beeswax, shaping it into a honeycomb of cells with the familiar six-sided, hexagonal form. The same cells serve double duty, used both to raise young and to store food.

Honey is made by collecting nectar from flowers and processing it for storage in the comb. When a forager finds a good source of food, she can tell her nestmates where it is through a movement called the waggle dance, which encodes the direction and distance of the find and lets the colony gather food efficiently.
