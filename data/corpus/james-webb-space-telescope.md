# James Webb Space Telescope

The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) is a large space observatory built to study the universe mainly in infrared light. Launched on December 25, 2021, it is a joint project of NASA, the European Space Agency, and the Canadian Space Agency, and it is usually described as the scientific successor to Hubble. Observing in the infrared lets it pick out very faint, distant objects, including some of the earliest galaxies, and see through dust that blocks visible light, which is why its instruments have to be kept extremely cold.

Its primary mirror is about 6.5 meters across, made of 18 hexagonal segments of lightweight beryllium, each coated with a thin layer of gold to reflect infrared well. A five-layer sunshield roughly the size of a tennis court blocks heat from the Sun, Earth, and Moon to keep the optics in shadow.

Instead of circling the Earth closely as Hubble does, JWST works near the second Sun-Earth Lagrange point, L2, about 1.5 million kilometers away. From there it can keep its sunshield pointed toward the Sun while it observes deep space.
