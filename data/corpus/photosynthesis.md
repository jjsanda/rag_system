# Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae, and some bacteria turn light into chemical energy stored in sugars. It is the main route by which the Sun's energy enters most food chains, and it produces the oxygen that many living things need to breathe. In plants it happens inside organelles called chloroplasts, whose green pigment, chlorophyll, absorbs light most strongly in the blue and red parts of the spectrum and reflects green, which is why leaves look green.

The process runs in two stages. In the light-dependent reactions, which take place in the thylakoid membranes, absorbed light splits water molecules and releases oxygen as a byproduct. In the light-independent reactions, the Calvin cycle, carbon dioxide is assembled into glucose in the stroma using the energy captured in the first stage.

Written as a single equation, six molecules of carbon dioxide and six of water, in the presence of light, yield one molecule of glucose and six of oxygen. The glucose is used as fuel and as a building block for other compounds, while the oxygen escapes into the atmosphere.
