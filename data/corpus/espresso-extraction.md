# Espresso Extraction

Espresso is a concentrated coffee made by forcing hot water under pressure through a compact bed of finely ground coffee. The result is a small, intense drink that serves as the base for many other coffee drinks; the mix of pressure, fine grounds, and short contact time is what sets it apart from simpler brewing methods.

To pull a shot, finely ground coffee goes into the filter basket of a handled tool called a portafilter and is pressed into an even, compact puck, a step called tamping. The machine then drives hot water through the puck. The usual parameters are:

- Pressure: about nine bars, roughly nine times normal atmospheric pressure.
- Water temperature: around 90 to 96 degrees Celsius.
- Shot time: about 25 to 30 seconds.
- Yield: roughly 25 to 30 milliliters for a single shot.

A good shot is capped by a layer of reddish-brown foam called crema, which forms as dissolved gases and oils emulsify under pressure. Because the extraction is pressurized, it pulls more dissolved solids and oils from the grounds than drip brewing does, which gives espresso its thick body and strong flavor.
