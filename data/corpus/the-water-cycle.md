# The Water Cycle

The water cycle, or hydrologic cycle, is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the Earth's surface. As it moves, water shifts between liquid, solid, and vapor, but the total amount on the planet stays essentially constant. Two things mainly drive it: energy from the Sun and the force of gravity.

## From the surface to the air

Evaporation occurs when the Sun heats liquid water in oceans, lakes, and rivers and turns it into vapor that rises into the atmosphere. Plants contribute as well: through transpiration, water drawn up by their roots is released as vapor from their leaves. Taken together, evaporation and transpiration are sometimes called evapotranspiration.

## Back to the ground

As the vapor rises and cools, it condenses into tiny droplets that gather into clouds. When the droplets grow heavy enough, they fall as precipitation: rain, snow, sleet, or hail. Water that reaches the ground may run off the surface back toward rivers and oceans, or soak in through infiltration to recharge the underground stores known as groundwater.

From rivers, oceans, soil, and groundwater the water is evaporated or taken up by plants again, and the cycle repeats with no fixed beginning or end.
